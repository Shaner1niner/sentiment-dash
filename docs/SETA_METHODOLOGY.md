# SETA Methodology

SETA is a behavioral market interpretation framework. It explains behavior beneath price by organizing price, sentiment, attention, participation, narrative context, regime state, indicator structure, and public-safe explanatory copy.

SETA is educational and analytical. It does not provide financial advice, investment recommendations, buy/sell signals, price targets, guarantees, or personalized guidance.

## Core Principle

Price shows what happened.

SETA helps explain what may be happening beneath price.

The framework treats sentiment, attention, source breadth, narrative behavior, and regime state as context layers. None of these layers is a standalone trading signal.

## Interpretation Layers

### Price

The price layer provides observable market structure: movement, range behavior, volatility, trend structure, compression, expansion, and relative behavior.

### Sentiment

The sentiment layer tracks how market participants appear to frame an asset, theme, or market environment. Sentiment can help identify optimism, fear, exhaustion, divergence, excessive consensus, or narrative fatigue.

Sentiment is context, not a prediction.

### Attention

The attention layer tracks how much crowd focus appears to be present. Attention can rise during breakouts, panic events, news cycles, speculative rotations, narrative shifts, and capitulation events.

Attention is not validation. High attention may reflect constructive participation, fear, hype, forced selling, spam, or late-cycle crowding.

### Authorship And Source Breadth

Authorship/source breadth is a trust layer inside attention analysis.

It asks whether participation appears to be distributed across a broader set of contributors or concentrated in a narrow source set. This helps distinguish organic diffusion from a small number of very loud voices.

SETA uses breadth as a check on attention quality:

- Broad breadth can improve confidence that attention is distributed.
- Narrow breadth can warn that raw attention may be concentrated or amplified.
- Source-limited breadth means the sample may not support a strong participation claim.

Breadth is most useful when author or source identity is available and reasonably complete. For X, API limits can make breadth incomplete, so SETA should treat the metric as confidence-weighted. For news, breadth should usually be read as outlet/source breadth rather than true crowd authorship because syndication, wire copy, editorial assignment, and repeated coverage can distort byline counts.

Breadth does not prove organic demand. It improves or reduces trust in the attention read.

### Narrative

The narrative layer describes the story structure around an asset, sector, theme, or market environment. Narratives can strengthen, weaken, fragment, reverse, rotate, become crowded, or detach from price.

### Regime

The regime layer describes the broader behavioral environment. The same sentiment or attention reading can mean different things in different regimes.

### Indicator Structure

Traditional indicators remain useful for timing, structure, and market context. SETA may reference moving averages, bands, oscillators, overlap structures, and timing panes as supporting evidence.

## Public-Safe Interpretation

Public-facing SETA copy should explain market context without crossing into financial advice.

It should avoid:

- buy/sell instructions
- price guarantees
- personalized advice
- unsupported predictions
- certainty language
- hype framing
- fear-based manipulation

Good SETA explanations should state what is happening, what evidence supports the read, what remains uncertain, and what should not be inferred.
